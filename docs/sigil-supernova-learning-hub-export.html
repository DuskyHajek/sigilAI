Overview & Investment Frameworks

The meta-layer — how to think before what to think

Before diving into the 7 specific themes, master the thinking frameworks Sigil applies across all bets. Disruptive technology investing requires a different toolkit than traditional value or growth investing. These mental models are the foundation for everything else.

11 concepts · 8 books · 5 courses · 5 voices · 6 mental models

Key concepts

TermDefinition
Gartner Hype Cycle5-stage tech adoption model: Innovation Trigger → Peak of Inflated Expectations → Trough of Disillusionment → Slope of Enlightenment → Plateau of Productivity. Sigil explicitly builds their AI portfolio to navigate this cycle — critical for timing entries and exits in each of the 7 themes.
Technology S-CurveAdoption curve: slow start (early adopters) → rapid growth (mainstream) → plateau (saturation). Stacked S-curves drive sequential investment waves. Knowing where a technology sits on its S-curve determines risk/reward.
First / Second / Third Order Effects1st order: AI GPU demand spikes. 2nd order: energy demand surges. 3rd order: nuclear power renaissance, copper mining boom. Sigil's entire 7-theme framework is second/third order thinking from the LLM breakthrough.
Innovator's DilemmaChristensen: incumbents rationally ignore disruptive tech because existing customers don't need it — until it's too late. The key lens for analyzing every SaaS company facing AI disruption.
Economic Moat (5 Types)Durable competitive advantage. Five types: (1) Network effects. (2) Switching costs. (3) Cost advantages. (4) Intangible assets (brands, patents). (5) Efficient scale (natural monopoly in limited markets).
Capital Cycle TheoryMarathon AM: high returns attract capital → overcapacity → returns fall → capital exits → returns recover. Explains why Sigil tracks 'cyclical PTSD' in semiconductor fabs — conservative capacity adds extend the up-cycle.
Asymmetric Risk / RewardBet structures where potential upside massively outweighs downside. Sigil's 2019 crypto thesis: risk 1 to win 50. Core skill: identifying these bets before consensus forms — by definition they are contrarian at entry.
Secular vs. Cyclical TrendSecular: long-duration structural change driven by fundamentals (AI adoption, demographics). Cyclical: short-duration waves driven by credit/inventory cycles (semiconductor boom/bust). Getting this wrong is the most common mistake in tech investing.
K-Shaped EconomyEconomic bifurcation where upper-income groups grow rapidly while lower-income groups stagnate. AI expected to widen this divide by automating middle-skill work. Political risk (UBI, anti-tech regulation) is a direct investment implication.
Path DependenceFuture outcomes shaped by historical decision sequences. Early platform choices (chip architecture, OS) produce winner-take-most dynamics that persist for decades. Invest in the platform that paths are being locked into.
Active vs. Passive InvestingPassive: track an index, capture market beta, low cost. Active: try to beat the market through research and selection. Active outperformance requires genuine informational or analytical edge. Sigil argues disruptive tech markets offer this edge.

Mental models

Inversion
Instead of 'How do we succeed?' ask 'What would guarantee failure?' Work backwards from catastrophic outcomes. Sigil's entire bear case section is exactly this — structured inversion of their own thesis. Apply it to every investment before committing.
Circle of Competence
Only invest where you have genuine, deep understanding. The boundary of your circle matters as much as its size. Sigil explicitly acknowledges their 'Day 0' position in AI — knowing what you don't know is itself an edge.
The Map is Not the Territory
Frameworks like the Gartner Hype Cycle are simplifications of reality. Reality will always surprise. Sigil calls their hype cycle placement 'honest but likely imprecise.' Holding models loosely while using them rigorously is a critical skill.
Opportunity Cost
Every investment forecloses alternatives. The relevant comparison for any AI infrastructure bet isn't zero — it's the best alternative use of that capital. Forces explicit prioritization across Sigil's 7 competing themes.
Second-Level Thinking (Howard Marks)
First-level thinking: 'This is a good company, buy it.' Second-level thinking: 'This is a good company, but everyone knows it, so the price already reflects that goodness — is there a reason the price is still wrong?' Apply before any investment decision: what does consensus think, and what might they be missing? Sigil's entire 7-theme framework is second-level: 'Everyone knows AI is big (first level). The bottleneck is physical, not digital, and most investors are missing it (second level).'
Confirmation Bias Audit
Before finalising any investment thesis, list the 3 strongest arguments AGAINST it. Force yourself to steelman the bear case. Sigil built 'Challenge the CIO' directly into their dashboard for exactly this reason — systematic anti-confirmation-bias process. The most dangerous time is when evidence is accumulating in your favour: that's when disconfirming signals are most likely to be ignored.

1. AI Infrastructure

Semiconductors, data centers, energy, and the physical layer of intelligence

The AI infrastructure buildout is the defining capex story of this decade. The most technically dense theme — but also the one with the clearest near-term bottlenecks and investment signals. Hyperscalers are spending $200B+ combined on AI capex in 2025, driving demand through 2027+.

16 concepts · 5 books · 5 courses · 5 voices · 4 mental models

Key concepts

TermDefinition
GPUGraphics Processing Unit. Originally for 3D graphics, now the primary AI training compute unit. Thousands of parallel cores. NVIDIA's H100 (~$30-40K each) and B200 dominate. NVIDIA controls ~80%+ of the AI GPU market. The most critical bottleneck in the AI stack since 2023.
ASIC / TPUApplication-Specific Integrated Circuit: a chip designed for exactly one task. More efficient than GPUs but less flexible. Google's TPU, AWS Trainium, Intel Gaudi are examples. Increasingly competing with NVIDIA in inference workloads.
TSMCTaiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company. World's most advanced chip foundry — manufactures chips for NVIDIA, Apple, AMD, Qualcomm. Controls ~90% of sub-5nm chip production. Located in Taiwan — the geopolitical single point of failure for the entire global technology supply chain.
ASML / EUV LithographyASML makes the only machines capable of printing chips at the most advanced nodes (sub-7nm) using Extreme Ultraviolet light. A single machine costs ~$200M. ASML has a global monopoly. The Netherlands export controls on ASML are a primary US-China semiconductor battleground.
HBM (High Bandwidth Memory)Memory stacked directly on processor dies, providing 10x+ the bandwidth of conventional DRAM. SK Hynix, Samsung, and Micron supply HBM. The 'memory wall' is one of AI's key hardware bottlenecks. HBM demand is a direct proxy for GPU demand.
HyperscalerMega-scale cloud and AI infrastructure operators: Amazon AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, Meta. Spending $200B+ combined on AI capex in 2025, driving the entire semiconductor and energy demand cycle.
NeocloudSpecialized AI-first data center operators renting GPU compute: CoreWeave, Lambda Labs, Vast.ai. Focused purely on AI compute. Bitcoin miners converting to Neoclouds is a capital-allocation arbitrage Sigil specifically highlights in their memo.
Fabless vs. FabFabless: companies design chips but outsource manufacturing (NVIDIA, Qualcomm, AMD, Apple). Fab: companies manufacture chips (TSMC, Samsung, Intel Foundry). The separation between design and manufacturing is the defining feature of the modern semiconductor industry.
Photonics / Silicon PhotonicsUsing light instead of electrical signals for data transmission within data centers. Optical interconnects offer massively higher bandwidth and lower power consumption than copper. Sigil notes photonics is entering a 'productive phase' — moving from R&D to deployment.
Rare Earth Elements17 metals (neodymium, dysprosium, terbium) critical for EV motors, missile guidance, wind turbines. China controls ~85% of global processing. A key geopolitical chokepoint. Sigil identifies 2-3 decades of Western underinvestment as creating a structural bottleneck.
Behind-the-Meter GenerationOn-site power generation bypassing the public grid — natural gas, solar, or small nuclear reactors. Hyperscalers increasingly use this for AI data centers to secure reliable, cheap power without grid capacity constraints. Microsoft's Three Mile Island deal is an early example.
Memory WallThe growing gap between processor speed and memory bandwidth. As GPUs get faster, they increasingly idle waiting for data. HBM partially addresses this. One of the key frontier hardware bottlenecks in AI scaling.
Semiconductor Supply ChainEDA software (Synopsys, Cadence) → chip design → lithography (ASML) → fab (TSMC) → advanced packaging (TSMC CoWoS, ASE) → test (Teradyne). Each step is a potential bottleneck. Advanced packaging is the newest bottleneck after GPUs.
Semiconductor Fab Capex CycleFab construction takes 3–5 years and $10–20B+ per leading-edge facility. After bust cycles, fabs add capacity conservatively ('cyclical PTSD') — extending up-cycles. High returns attract capex → overcapacity → returns fall. Marathon AM's capital cycle theory applied directly to TSMC, Intel Foundry, and Samsung fab investment decisions.
SLM / Edge InferenceSmall Language Models running on-device (phones, cars, robots) rather than data centers. Microsoft Phi, Google Gemma, Meta Llama. Sigil identifies edge inference as the 'opposite trend' — both centralized buildout and edge inference matter simultaneously.
PUE (Power Usage Effectiveness)Ratio of total data center energy to IT equipment energy. Industry average ~1.5; hyperscalers target 1.1-1.2. AI data centers are more power-intensive, driving innovation in liquid cooling and behind-the-meter generation.

Mental models

Picks and Shovels
During a gold rush, sell picks and shovels — not gold. The infrastructure enablers (ASML, TSMC, NVIDIA) often have more durable economics than the AI companies they serve. The constraint captures the margin: whoever controls the bottleneck extracts the value.
Bottleneck Theory (Theory of Constraints)
Every system has one binding constraint. In AI: 2022 = software (no good models). 2023-24 = GPU supply. 2025 = HBM and advanced packaging. 2026+ = energy and power delivery. Identifying the current bottleneck tells you exactly where value is concentrating today.
Geopolitical Risk Layering
Taiwan Strait risk exists on a spectrum: trade tensions → export controls → blockade → invasion. Model each scenario's impact on the portfolio separately. A partial TSMC disruption (export controls) is very different from a full invasion scenario.
Bottleneck Migration
In any constrained system, fixing one bottleneck immediately reveals the next. AI infrastructure bottleneck timeline: 2022 = models (no capable models existed). 2023-24 = GPU supply. 2025 = HBM and advanced packaging. 2026+ = energy and power delivery. Investment implication: don't buy the previous bottleneck — buy what becomes the next bottleneck BEFORE consensus identifies it. The edge is in early bottleneck prediction, not late bottleneck confirmation.

2. Application Layer

LLMs, AI agents, SaaS disruption, and the software economy repricing

The application layer is where AI touches every business and consumer. The key insight: moats built on UI/UX friction are dying; moats built on proprietary data, distribution, and regulatory lock-in are strengthening. This is the theme with the most nuanced investment approach — not simply 'buy AI, sell SaaS.'

13 concepts · 6 books · 4 courses · 5 voices · 3 mental models

Key concepts

TermDefinition
LLM (Large Language Model)Neural networks trained on vast text using transformer architecture. Key properties: in-context learning (learns from examples in prompt), emergent capabilities (appear at scale), hallucination tendency (generates false information confidently). GPT-4, Claude 3, Gemini Ultra are frontier models.
Foundation ModelA large model trained on broad data adapted for specific tasks via fine-tuning or prompting. The 'platform layer' of AI. OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta are the foundation model labs. The race: best foundation = most compute + highest-quality data.
RAG (Retrieval Augmented Generation)Architecture connecting an LLM to an external database. Before generating, the model retrieves relevant context. Reduces hallucinations, enables private/proprietary data use, avoids constant retraining. The dominant enterprise AI architecture in 2024-2026.
AI AgentSoftware entity autonomously perceiving its environment, making decisions, and taking actions to achieve goals without continuous human direction. The shift from AI as tool (you use it) to AI as worker (it does the job).
Agentic WorkflowA sequence of AI agent actions completing a complex, multi-step task autonomously. E.g., 'research competitors, write analysis, send report' — all without human handoffs. The most disruptive paradigm in enterprise software since cloud computing.
Software Moat TaxonomyFour types in decreasing AI-resistance: (1) Proprietary data — datasets no competitor can replicate. (2) Regulatory/distribution lock-in. (3) Switching costs — workflow migration is painful. (4) UI/UX friction — DYING. AI agents collapse the friction that justified paying for many SaaS tools.
SaaS Metrics: ARR / NRRARR (Annual Recurring Revenue): subscription revenue annualized. NRR (Net Revenue Retention): revenue from existing customers including expansion minus churn. >120% NRR means customers expand spend over time — one of the strongest product-market fit signals.
Token Budget / AI OPEXThe computational cost of AI inference is becoming a major operating expense for companies. As compute becomes a real budget line, low-value AI tasks are priced out or shifted to open-source models. Resets the business case for many SaaS products.
Aggregation TheoryBen Thompson: platforms aggregating consumer demand extract value from suppliers. Google, Amazon, Apple built moats through demand aggregation. AI agents may disrupt even these aggregators by navigating directly to content — disintermediating the aggregators themselves.
Agentic PaymentsAbility of AI agents to transact value autonomously — paying for services, receiving payments, managing budgets. A key infrastructure need for the agentic economy. Where Sigil's crypto expertise directly translates.
Multi-agent SystemNetworks of AI agents, each specialized for subtasks, coordinating to complete complex workflows. Analogous to a company org chart. The architecture for serious enterprise AI deployment at scale.
Fine-tuningAdditional training of a pre-trained foundation model on domain-specific data to optimize for a specific task. Creates more durable product differentiation but requires proprietary data and engineering resources.
Dead Internet Theory (Operational)The observation that most internet content is now AI-generated or bot-amplified. Implications: every platform dependent on authentic human content faces structural degradation of its value proposition.

Mental models

Aggregation Theory Applied to AI
Whoever controls consumer demand (the aggregator) captures value from suppliers. Key question for every AI company: are they aggregating demand, or being disaggregated by someone at a higher layer? Foundation model labs may be disaggregated by OS-level AI (Apple, Microsoft) which is disaggregated by hardware (NVIDIA).
Moat Classification Test
Before every software investment, classify the moat: (1) UI/UX friction — dying, avoid or short. (2) Workflow switching costs — at risk but time-delayed. (3) Proprietary data — resilient, seek. (4) Regulatory/distribution lock-in — growing, priority. This matrix determines sizing and time horizons.
The Second-Derivative Bet
Rather than picking which AI application wins (first-order), bet on the infrastructure all applications need: orchestration layers, agent security, inference optimization. Picks-and-shovels at the application layer — they win regardless of which specific apps succeed.

3. Robotics & Automation

Automation of physical work — the last frontier of intelligent systems

70% of the global economy is still physical. Robotics has been overhyped for decades, but AI is finally changing the capability curve. The near-term opportunity isn't humanoids — it's specialized robots in high-labor, lower-margin industries where AI dramatically changes the ROI math. Sigil's portfolio company Budbreak Innovations (vineyard disease detection) exemplifies this approach.

10 concepts · 4 books · 3 courses · 3 voices · 2 mental models

Key concepts

TermDefinition
Degrees of Freedom (DoF)The number of independent movements a robotic system can make. Human arm: ~7 DoF. Industrial robot: typically 6 DoF. More DoF enables more complex manipulation but requires more sophisticated control algorithms and more expensive hardware.
SLAMSimultaneous Localization and Mapping. Algorithm allowing a robot to build a map of an unknown environment while simultaneously tracking its own location. Core algorithm enabling autonomous navigation — now augmented with deep learning for much more robust performance.
World ModelAn AI's internal representation of the physical environment, used for planning and prediction. The critical missing piece in pre-AI robotics. Robots now build these dynamically from sensor data (cameras, LiDAR, IMUs).
End-EffectorThe device at the end of a robotic arm: grippers, welders, spray nozzles, surgical tools. Dexterous end-effectors for delicate tasks remain a hard engineering problem — and a key hardware investment opportunity.
Collaborative Robot (Cobot)Robots designed to work safely alongside humans without barriers. Lower cost ($50-100K vs. $500K+ for traditional industrial robots), easily reprogrammable. Universal Robots pioneered this category. Key for services and light manufacturing.
Actuator TypesThe 'muscles' of a robot. Electric actuators (servo motors, most common) use rare earth magnets — a supply chain link. The shift from hydraulic to all-electric is a major materials demand trend.
Humanoid RobotBipedal robots shaped like humans. Companies: Figure AI, Tesla Optimus, Agility Robotics, Boston Dynamics Atlas. The most hyped category. Sigil is cautious here — humanoids only make engineering sense where environments are built specifically for human form factors.
Agricultural RoboticsSpecialized robots for farming: planting, harvesting, pruning, spraying, disease detection. Large TAM ($100B+), high labor intensity, narrow AI requirement (not generalist AGI). The investable near-term robotics frontier Sigil prefers over humanoids.
Local Inference UnitOn-board AI processor enabling real-time robot decision-making without cloud connectivity. Critical for latency-sensitive applications: self-driving, surgical robotics, manufacturing QC. Links the robotics theme to the AI inference hardware theme.
Automation Paradox / Jevons ParadoxCounterintuitive finding that partial automation can increase total demand for human labor by creating new job categories (supervising, maintaining, programming automated systems). Historical data from manufacturing automation supports this pattern.

Mental models

Form Factor Follows Function
Humanoid robots are engineering optimal only where environments are built for human form factors and cannot be modified. For agriculture, warehousing, and mining, purpose-built specialized robots are more practical, cheaper, and closer to commercial viability. Invest in practical form factors first.
Hardware BOM Analysis

4. Future of Warfare

Autonomous systems, drone economics, and the defence tech renaissance

NATO budgets are rising at Cold War rates. Ukraine demonstrated autonomous loitering munitions permanently restructured warfare economics — a $20K drone destroying a $3M tank is a 150:1 cost asymmetry. Mastering this theme requires understanding defence procurement, the Innovator's Dilemma in military technology, and the critical materials underpinning modern weapons.

13 concepts · 5 books · 4 courses · 4 voices · 4 mental models

Key concepts

TermDefinition
C4ISRCommand, Control, Communications, Computers, Intelligence, Surveillance and Reconnaissance. The nervous system of modern military operations. AI is transforming every layer — from real-time battlefield data aggregation to autonomous targeting decision-support.
Loitering MunitionA drone that 'loiters' over an area autonomously until it identifies a target, then strikes. Also called 'suicide drone.' Lancet (Russian), Shahed (Iranian/Russian), Switchblade (US). Ukraine's use against Russian tanks redefined cost-exchange ratios permanently.
Counter-UAV (C-UAV)Systems to detect, track, and neutralize enemy drones. Methods: RF jamming, GPS spoofing, kinetic (missiles, cannons), directed energy (lasers), interceptor drones. One of the fastest-growing defence categories globally as drone proliferation accelerates.
Directed Energy Weapon (DEW)Weapons using focused energy instead of projectiles: lasers (HELIOS by Raytheon), high-power microwave. Key advantages: speed-of-light delivery, very low cost per shot (dollars of electricity vs. $1M+ missiles), essentially unlimited 'ammunition' as long as power is available.
NATO 2% GDP TargetNATO members' commitment to spend 2%+ of GDP on defence. Many European countries now exceeding this — Germany targeting 2-3%, Poland at 4%+. Creates structural, multi-year demand expansion for European and NATO-aligned defence companies.
Cost-Exchange RatioRatio of attacker cost to defender cost. Historical norm: slight defender advantage. Ukraine showed 150:1 attacker advantage with loitering munitions vs. tanks. This drives the entire NATO force structure rethink toward drone swarms and C-UAV.
Innovator's Dilemma in DefenceLarge prime contractors (Lockheed, Raytheon, BAE) are optimized for slow, complex, expensive programs and cannot produce fast, cheap, software-first solutions. Procurement bureaucracy is their moat. Creates the gap that startups (Palantir, Anduril, Shield AI) are filling.
Attritable SystemsLow-cost, expendable autonomous platforms designed to be lost in combat without strategic impact — the opposite of $13B aircraft carriers and $3M tanks. Ukraine demonstrated that attritable $20K drones can destroy high-value assets. Kratos, Anduril, and loitering munition makers express this thesis.
OSINT (Open Source Intelligence)Intelligence from public sources: commercial satellite imagery, social media, commercial databases. AI dramatically amplifies OSINT capabilities. Ukraine used commercial OSINT to track Russian forces in near-real-time — satellite intelligence is no longer a state monopoly.
Dual-use TechnologyTechnology with both civilian and military applications. Most deep tech today is dual-use: drones, satellite imagery, AI, advanced manufacturing. Regulatory treatment varies significantly by jurisdiction and export destination.
Critical Materials for DefenceTungsten (armor-piercing rounds), rare earth elements (missile guidance, radar), titanium (airframes), lithium (autonomous system batteries). China controls processing of most of these. Western reshoring is both a strategic necessity and a direct investment opportunity.
Grey Zone WarfareHostile activities below the threshold of conventional war: cyberattacks, election interference, economic coercion, proxy conflicts, information warfare, undersea cable sabotage. The primary mode of great power conflict short of direct war.
ISRIntelligence, Surveillance, Reconnaissance. Historically expensive (spy planes, KH satellites). Now increasingly democratized via commercial satellites and OSINT. ISR quality — seeing the battlefield better — is increasingly decisive in modern conflict.

Three converging forces — collapsed launch costs (SpaceX), strategic competition (US-China), and AI-driven demand — are creating the first commercially viable space economy since the Apollo era. Low Earth Orbit (LEO)200–2,000 km altitude. Where Starlink, ISS, and most Earth observation satellites operate. Short orbital period (~90 min), relatively low launch cost. SpaceX's primary commercial frontier. The 'hottest' orbital real estate in the New Space era.

7. Adversarial AI & Cybersecurity

The structural security arms race — AI attacks, AI defends

AI dramatically expands both the attack surface (more agents, more APIs, more entry points) and attacker sophistication (automated exploit generation, deepfake social engineering). Cybersecurity is Sigil's cleanest exception to the 'AI won't help new software startups' thesis — the threat landscape genuinely did not exist before AI. Sigil has partnered with Tachles to identify the best opportunities here.

12 concepts · 4 books · 4 courses · 4 voices · 3 mental models

Key concepts

TermDefinition
Zero Trust Architecture (ZTA)Security model assuming no user or device is inherently trusted, even inside the network perimeter. Every access request is verified explicitly. Contrast with legacy 'castle and moat' model. Zero trust is driving major enterprise security spending — Zscaler, CrowdStrike, Okta are key beneficiaries.
Attack Surface Management (ASM)Continuous identification, classification, and monitoring of all assets exposed to attack: domains, IPs, APIs, cloud services. As AI agents proliferate, attack surfaces expand faster than human teams can track manually. ASM tools are among the fastest-growing security categories.
Prompt InjectionAdversarial technique hijacking an AI agent's behavior by inserting malicious instructions into inputs. A malicious web page can instruct a browsing agent to exfiltrate user data. One of the hardest unsolved security problems in AI systems.
AI-Powered Attack AutomationAI systems that autonomously discover vulnerabilities, craft phishing campaigns, generate polymorphic malware, or scale social engineering at machine speed. Dramatically lowers the cost and skill floor for sophisticated attacks — expanding the threat surface faster than defender headcount can scale.
Model Poisoning / Data PoisoningCorrupting an AI model's training data to introduce backdoors, biases, or specific misbehaviors. Particularly dangerous for models trained on open internet data. If an attacker can influence training data, they create 'sleeper' vulnerabilities.
Deepfakes / Synthetic MediaAI-generated images, video, or audio indistinguishable from authentic content. Applications: fraud, disinformation, CEO voice impersonation for wire transfer fraud. Driving demand for digital content provenance (C2PA standard) and liveness detection.
Digital Identity VerificationProving a user is human and who they claim to be. Current methods (SMS OTP, selfie checks) are increasingly defeated by AI-generated synthetic identities. Next-gen: hardware attestation, biometric liveness detection, decentralized identity. Sigil's specific investment focus in this theme.
Nation-State APTAdvanced Persistent Threat: sophisticated, long-duration state-sponsored cyberattacks. China's APT41 (economic espionage), Russia's Sandworm (infrastructure attacks), North Korea's Lazarus (financial theft). Primary demand driver for enterprise security spending.
Supply Chain AttackCompromising a software vendor to infect all downstream customers. SolarWinds (2020): 18,000 organizations including US Treasury. XZ Utils backdoor (2024): nearly compromised global Linux systems. Highest-leverage attack vector — one breach reaches millions.
SOC AutomationAI-driven automation of Security Operations Center functions: alert triage, log analysis, threat hunting. Most tier-1 SOC work is repetitive and AI-automatable. Reducing SOC cost while improving response speed is a key AI + cybersecurity convergence use case.
C2PA (Content Credentials Standard)Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity. Standard for cryptographically signing digital content at creation — the technical solution to deepfakes. Adobe, Microsoft, BBC are members.
AI Alignment (Applied)Ensuring AI systems pursue goals aligned with human values. Applied security: preventing harmful outputs, jailbreaks, and social engineering attacks via AI. Both short-term (jailbreaks) and long-term (power-seeking) dimensions are relevant to cybersecurity investment thesis.

Mental models

Offense-Defense Asymmetry
In cybersecurity, attackers need to find one vulnerability; defenders must protect every single one. AI further tilts this asymmetry by automating attack generation at scale. This is why the cybersecurity market grows faster than almost any other software category in downturns — demand is structurally non-discretionary.
Attack Surface × Sophistication = Threat Level
Threat level is the product of attack surface (every new AI agent, API, connected device adds surface — all growing fast) and attacker sophistication (AI-powered — also growing fast). Both variables compound simultaneously. This is why the cybersecurity TAM is expanding faster than any macroeconomic headwind can slow.
Regulation as Moat
In cybersecurity and AI safety, regulatory requirements can become a moat for incumbents. FedRAMP authorisation takes 12-24 months. GDPR compliance infrastructure requires legal teams and technical investment. SOC 2 Type II audits take 6-12 months. New entrants face the same compliance burden regardless of technical quality. Companies with established regulatory compliance in healthcare (HIPAA), finance (SOX), or defence (CMMC) have a genuine structural advantage that AI alone cannot replicate — compliance is time, not just money.
